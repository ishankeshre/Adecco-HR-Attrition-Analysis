# Adecco HR Attrition Analysis

## Project Overview

This project analyses employee data from Adecco India to understand employee attrition patterns and identify key factors associated with employee turnover.

The analysis was performed using Microsoft Excel, including Pivot Tables, formulas, data analysis, and an interactive dashboard. The project examines employee demographics, job characteristics, compensation, satisfaction, work-life balance, tenure, overtime, and other workforce-related factors.

## Business Objective

The objective of this analysis is to help HR teams:

* Measure the overall employee attrition rate
* Identify departments and job roles with higher attrition
* Understand demographic patterns among employees who leave
* Analyse the impact of overtime, job involvement, compensation, and stock options
* Examine employee satisfaction and work-life balance
* Identify factors most strongly associated with employee attrition
* Support data-driven employee retention strategies

## Dataset

The dataset contains **1,470 employee records** with information covering:

* Employee demographics

## Key Insights

### Overall Attrition

* **1,470 employees** were analysed.
* **237 employees left** the company.
* **1,233 employees stayed.**
* Overall attrition rate was **16.12%**.

### Department Attrition

* **Sales** had the highest attrition rate at **20.63%**.
* Human Resources had an attrition rate of **19.05%**.
* Research & Development had the lowest attrition rate among the three departments at **13.84%**.

### Age

* Employees who left had an average age of **33.61 years**.
* Employees who stayed had an average age of **37.56 years**.
* This indicates that employees who left were, on average, younger than those who stayed.

### Gender

* Female employee attrition rate: **14.80%**
* Male employee attrition rate: **17.01%**

Male employees had a moderately higher attrition rate in this dataset.

### Compensation

* Average monthly income of employees who left: **4,787**
* Average monthly income of employees who stayed: **6,833**

Employees who left also had a lower average job level, suggesting a relationship between seniority, compensation, and attrition.

### Overtime

Overtime showed one of the clearest differences in attrition:

* Employees working overtime: **30.53% attrition**
* Employees not working overtime: **10.44% attrition**

Employees working overtime had almost **three times** the attrition rate of employees who did not work overtime.

### Job Involvement

Attrition decreased as job involvement increased:

* Job involvement 1: **33.73%**
* Job involvement 2: **18.93%**
* Job involvement 3: **14.40%**
* Job involvement 4: **9.03%**

This suggests that lower employee involvement is associated with substantially higher attrition.

### Marital Status

* Single employees: **25.53% attrition**
* Married employees: **12.48%**
* Divorced employees: **10.09%**

Single employees had the highest attrition rate among the three groups.

### Stock Options

Employees with no stock options had an attrition rate of **24.41%**, compared with:

* Level 1: **9.40%**
* Level 2: **7.59%**
* Level 3: **17.65%**

The results indicate a strong association between stock option availability and employee retention.

### Key Factors Associated with Attrition

Correlation analysis identified the strongest relationships with attrition as:

1. **Total Working Years**
2. **Job Level**
3. **Years in Current Role**
4. **Monthly Income**
5. **Age**

The correlations are associations within the dataset and should not be interpreted as proof that these factors directly cause attrition.

## Conclusion

The analysis shows that employee attrition at Adecco India is associated with several workforce characteristics, particularly overtime, job involvement, compensation, job level, tenure, and stock options.

The most notable finding is the large difference in attrition between employees who work overtime and those who do not. Lower job involvement, lower compensation, and absence of stock options are also associated with higher attrition.

These insights can help HR teams identify higher-risk employee groups and focus retention efforts on areas such as workload management, employee engagement, career progression, and compensation.
